# Atlas Pilot Playbook — Landing and Onboarding Customer #1

Owner: Head of Growth. Status: living document. Scope: the **first** pilot only — everything here optimizes for learning and a referenceable win, not revenue.

## 1. Positioning

Atlas is the feasibility engine for Israeli urban renewal (התחדשות עירונית). Every developer, yazam, and shamai evaluating a pinui-binui (פינוי-בינוי) or TAMA 38 successor project today runs the same ritual: someone rebuilds an Excel model from scratch — unit mix, rights per the taba (תב"ע), build costs, eviction economics — with no versioning, no sensitivity analysis, and no way to compare scenarios. The timing is forcing a change: the TAMA 38 framework is sunsetting and municipalities are pushing volume into pinui-binui and mitchamim (מתחמים), where deals are bigger, longer, and far less forgiving of a spreadsheet error. Atlas imports real taba/PIO data (Hebrew encodings included), runs IRR/NPV/payback and sensitivity in seconds, and shows the before/after in 3D — so a feasibility answer that took a week of analyst time takes an afternoon, and every scenario is comparable and defensible in front of a bank, a shamai, or a diyarim assembly.

## 2. Where to Find Pilot #1 (prioritized)

| # | Channel | Why / How | Effort |
|---|---------|-----------|--------|
| 1 | **Warm intros** | Founders' and investors' networks into mid-size developers (5–30 active projects) and yazamut firms. One intro to a VP Business Development or feasibility analyst beats any outbound. Ask every contact: "who builds your feasibility Excels?" | Low, start day 1 |
| 2 | **Shamaim & feasibility consultants** | Appraisers (שמאים) and economic consultants do this analysis for many developers — one convinced consultant is a channel, not just a customer. Target via the Land Appraisers Association (לשכת שמאי המקרקעין) events. | Low-medium |
| 3 | **Municipal urban-renewal administrations** | Minhelot hitchadshut ironit (מנהלות התחדשות עירונית) in Tel Aviv, Haifa, Ramat Gan, Bat Yam evaluate developer proposals and run their own feasibility checks. Slower sales cycle but strong credibility; also municipal innovation programs (e.g., city accelerators). Pursue as pilot #2–3 candidate, not #1. | Medium |
| 4 | **Conferences & forums** | Urban renewal conferences (כנסי התחדשות עירונית — TheMarker/Globes real-estate events, Merkaz HaBniya), LinkedIn groups and WhatsApp/Facebook developer forums. Use for pipeline building and to source warm intros, not for closing #1. | Medium, ongoing |

**Ideal pilot profile:** a developer with 3+ pinui-binui projects in evaluation, an in-house analyst who owns the Excel today (our champion), and pain fresh enough to commit time — not the biggest logo we can reach.

## 3. Pilot Offer Structure

- **Price: free**, explicitly framed as a design-partner arrangement, not a discount. Rationale: with zero references, price negotiation adds friction without validating willingness to pay (we validate that separately, §5).
- **Duration: 8 weeks**, hard end date set at kickoff. An open-ended free pilot never converts and never ends.
- **Scope:** up to 5 real projects imported, unlimited scenarios, web app + reports. Mobile app excluded (not needed for the feasibility desk workflow).
- **What Atlas gets in return (written into a one-page pilot agreement):**
  1. **Feedback commitment:** a named champion, 1 hour/week review session, responses to async questions within 2 business days.
  2. **Case-study rights:** approval to publish an anonymized case study; logo/named rights negotiated at pilot end contingent on results.
  3. **Testimonial + 2 reference calls** if success criteria are met.
  4. **First-right conversation** on a paid annual agreement in week 7 — they agree up front to hear the offer.
- **Success criteria agreed at kickoff (example):** 3 real projects fully modeled in Atlas; at least one go/no-go decision informed by an Atlas simulation; champion agrees output is bank/shamai-presentable.

## 4. Onboarding Journey (8 weeks)

| Week | Milestone | Details |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **0 (pre-kickoff)** | Data audit | Get 1–2 sample files (taba CSV/XLSX exports, their Excel models). Dry-run the import internally — cp1255 encoding, column mapping (מספר תיק, יח"ד קיים/מוצע…) — so kickoff has zero import surprises. |
| **1** | Kickoff (90 min) | Champion + sponsor present. Agree success criteria, weekly cadence, named contacts. Create accounts, import project #1 live in the meeting. First "wow": their real project on the map. |
| **2** | First simulation | Model project #1 end-to-end: scenario setup (unit mix, costs, prices), run IRR/NPV/payback + sensitivity. Side-by-side against their existing Excel — reconcile every discrepancy; this builds trust in the numbers or exposes our bugs early. |
| **3** | Review #1 + projects 2–3 | Champion imports projects 2–3 **themselves** (we observe — this is our usability test). Log every friction point. |
| **4** | Mid-pilot checkpoint | 30 min with the sponsor, not just the champion. Show usage stats and modeled projects. Kill/fix list from weeks 1–3 triaged with engineering. |
| **5** | Real decision workflow | Push to use Atlas in a live setting: a go/no-go discussion, bank deck, or shamai review using Atlas reports (PDF/XLSX export). This is the moment that generates the case study. |
| **6** | Review #2 + pricing interviews | Structured pricing conversation (§5). Second reconciliation pass on numbers. |
| **7** | Conversion conversation | Present the paid offer per the agreement. Whatever the answer, capture *why* in detail. |
| **8** | Wrap-up + case study | Retro with champion + sponsor. Testimonial ask, case-study draft, reference-call agreement. Decide: convert, extend (only with payment), or end. |

Weekly ritual throughout: 1-hour session (screen-share, their data), same day/time every week; async channel (WhatsApp — it's Israel) for questions.

## 5. Pricing Hypothesis to Validate (not to charge)

**Hypothesis:** per-seat SaaS underprices the value; **per-active-project or tiered project-count pricing** matches how developers think (a pinui-binui feasibility mistake costs millions of NIS; consultants charge tens of thousands per feasibility study).

During the pilot, learn:
1. **Value anchor** — what do they pay today for an external feasibility opinion, and how many analyst-hours does one Excel model cost them?
2. **Budget owner** — does this come from a project budget (easy, large) or IT/software budget (hard, small)? Who signs?
3. **Willingness-to-pay range** — Van Westendorp-style questions in week 6: at what annual price is Atlas "expensive but worth it" vs. "too cheap to trust"? Test reactions to ₪3–5K/month/company vs. ₪1.5–2.5K per active project.
4. **Packaging** — do they value the 3D/report layer (sales tool for diyarim assemblies) enough for a premium tier, or is the engine the whole value?

## 6. Case Study & Reference Plan (post-pilot)

- **Week 8–9:** draft a 1-page case study (Hebrew primary, English version after): baseline (Excel, X days per feasibility), what changed, one concrete number ("modeled 4 projects, 12 scenarios; feasibility turnaround from 5 days to 1"). Get written approval.
- **Assets:** quote + headshot from champion, anonymized screenshots with their real (approved) project, 2-minute screen-capture demo of their workflow.
- **Distribution:** LinkedIn (founder + company page), the developer forums from §2, and as the centerpiece of the next 10 outbound conversations.
- **Reference machine:** champion commits to 2 reference calls; ask directly for 3 warm intros to peer developers — the Israeli urban-renewal world is small and intro-driven; pilot #1's real output is pilots #2–4.

## 7. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Signal | Mitigation |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **Pilot goes quiet** | Missed weekly session, no logins for 7+ days | Hard weekly cadence with a standing invite; usage monitoring with a "no login in 5 days" internal alert; sponsor (not just champion) looped in at weeks 1 and 4; pre-agreed end date makes drift visible. If two sessions are missed — direct reset conversation or end the pilot and recycle the slot. |
| **Data too messy to import** | Week 0 dry-run fails; nonstandard Excel formats, missing taba fields | Week 0 audit *before* kickoff is the gate — never discover this live. Budget engineering time for one custom mapping; offer white-glove manual import of the first 2 projects so the pilot never stalls on plumbing. If data is fundamentally unusable, disqualify politely and take the lesson to product. |
| **Champion leaves / reorg** | Notice period, sudden calendar silence | Multi-thread from day 1: sponsor at kickoff and week 4, second user trained by week 3. Value evidence stored in Atlas (their projects, their scenarios) so the asset survives the person. |
| **Numbers don't match their Excel** | Week 2 reconciliation gaps | Treat as top-priority: every discrepancy resolved and explained (their formula error vs. our bug — both are wins if handled transparently). Golden-fixture tests in `services/analytics` back us up. |
| **Pilot succeeds but won't pay** | Week 7 stall | Pricing conversation contractually scheduled, not sprung; success criteria tied to business value (a real decision made), not feature usage; if they still balk, extract maximum reference value and treat the WTP data as the pilot's ROI. |
